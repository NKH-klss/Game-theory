Sprague-Grundy Theorem & Game Theory

Mathematical principles behind Wythoff's Nim & Grid Games

1. Impartial Games & Normal Play Convention

All games in this engine (Wythoff's Queen, Rook Nim, King Nim, Pawn Nim) are Impartial Games under the Normal Play Convention:

  • Both players have identical available moves from any position.
  • No hidden information or randomness.
  • The player who makes the last legal move to (0,0) wins the game!

2. Sprague-Grundy Theorem & MEX Function

By the Sprague-Grundy Theorem, every position $(x,y)$ in an impartial game is equivalent to a single Nim-pile of size $G(x,y)$.

G(x, y) = MEX({ G(x', y') | (x', y') is a valid 1-step move from (x,y) })

Where MEX (Minimum Excluded Value) is the smallest non-negative integer $\{0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ not present in the set of reachable Grundy values.

3. P-Positions (Safe/Winning Target) vs N-Positions

P-Position (Previous Player Wins)

Equivalent to G(x,y) = 0. The player who just moved left the game in a winning state for themselves. Any move from a P-position will always lead to an N-position ($G > 0$).

N-Position (Next Player Wins)

Equivalent to G(x,y) > 0. The current player whose turn it is can always make a move to reach a P-position ($G=0$) and secure victory!

4. Wythoff's Nim & Beatty's Theorem (Golden Ratio $\phi$)

In Wythoff's Queen Nim, the P-positions ($G=0$) form a famous mathematical spectrum governed by the Golden Ratio $\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.6180339887...$

P-positions = ( ⌊n · φ⌋, ⌊n · φ²⌋ ) for n = 0, 1, 2, 3...
Pairs: (0,0), (1,2), (3,5), (4,7), (6,10), (8,13)...

By Beatty's Theorem, these two sequences partition the natural numbers completely without overlap!
