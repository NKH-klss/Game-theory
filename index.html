Combinatorial Game Theory Mathematical Principles

Sprague-Grundy & Impartial Games
1. Grundy Value & MEX

Every position $(x,y)$ in an impartial game under normal play has a Grundy value equal to the Minimum Excluded value (MEX) of all reachable positions:

G(x,y) = MEX({ G(x',y') | (x',y') is reachable })
2. P-Positions vs N-Positions

P-Positions ($G = 0$): Previous player wins. Moving from $G=0$ always leads to $G > 0$.
N-Positions ($G > 0$): Next player wins. There is always at least one move to a $G=0$ position.

3. Golden Ratio ($\phi$) in Wythoff

Wythoff's P-positions $(a_n, b_n)$ follow Beatty's Theorem and the Golden Ratio $\phi = \frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2} \approx 1.618$:

a_n = ⌊n · φ⌋, b_n = ⌊n · φ²⌋ = a_n + n
4. Nim-Sum & XOR Addition

In 2-Pile Rook Nim, moves operate independently per axis. By Bouton's Theorem, the Grundy value equals the bitwise XOR sum:

G(x,y) = x ⊕ y ⇒ P-Positions when x = y

COMBINATORIAL GAME THEORY COMPENDIUM

Normal Play Convention & Terminal State

In standard normal play combinatorial game theory, the player who makes the last legal move wins. Moving the token to $(0,0)$ leaves the board with zero legal remaining moves, sealing an immediate victory.

Why Sprague-Grundy Works

By the Sprague-Grundy Theorem, any finite impartial game under normal play is isomorphic to a single-pile Nim game of size $G(x,y)$. The MEX operation guarantees that a state with $G(x,y) = k$ can transition to states with Grundy values $0, 1, \dots, k-1$.

Wythoff's Game & The Golden Ratio Sequence

In Wythoff's Queen Nim, the safe $G=0$ positions are pairs $(\lfloor n\phi \rfloor, \lfloor n\phi^2 \rfloor)$: $(0,0), (1,2), (3,5), (4,7), (6,10), (8,13), \dots$ and their reflections. No two pairs share the same row, column, or diagonal!
